How it works

1 · Pull

Every construction job in the five boroughs starts as a job filing with the Department of Buildings, in the DOB NOW system. The city publishes those filings every day, with the owner, the architect or engineer, the size and a description. Follow-on filings for the same job (plumbing, sprinklers and so on) are folded into the first one.

Permits come from a second city dataset. Where a licensed general contractor holds a permit on the job, the page names them.

2 · Cut

  • Private work. City agencies, NYCHA, the School Construction Authority and other government owners come out. They go to public bid. Withdrawn filings come out too.
  • A real job. At least 5,000 sq ft of work, 5 new dwelling units, or $500,000 of filed cost. Units only count where the job adds them: an office fit-out in a 300-unit tower is not a 300-unit job.
  • Local-builder size. Under 250,000 sq ft and under $50M.
  • Still open. Filed inside the window you pick and not yet signed off. The stage setting narrows it to jobs before a permit, or jobs with a permit.

3 · Stage

Each job's stage comes from its live DOB status: filed, in plan review (including objections), approved, permit issued, signed off. That replaces guessing: you can see how far along a job really is.

4 · Rank

Score = freshness (up to 35, falling to 0 at the edge of the window) + size (up to 35, log scale of estimated value) + type of work (new building 25, new building with parts remaining 22, major alteration 15, alteration 10) + 5 for housing units.

5 · Estimate

Alterations carry a cost the owner filed with DOB, and the page uses it. Owners often file low, so treat it as a floor. DOB does not collect a cost on new buildings, so those use floor area × cost per sq ft (default $500, change it on the Call list tab). Each trade's slice is a rule-of-thumb share.

These are rough numbers for qualifying leads, not estimates to bid from.

6 · Who to contact

The filing names the owner, the architect or engineer of record and the filing representative. DOB does not publish their phone numbers or emails in this data, so each name links to a search. Every project also links to the DOB building profile and the zoning map for the lot.
